1. What Is Huawei?
Before diving into Huawei's AI products, it helps to know the company itself. Huawei Technologies
was founded in 1987 in Shenzhen, China, by Ren Zhengfei, a former army engineer, starting out by
manufacturing phone switches. Today it is one of the largest technology companies in the world, and
one of the more unusual ones: it has never gone public. Huawei is privately owned by its own
employees rather than by outside shareholders.

Huawei's AI push is not a side project bolted onto a phone company. It sits inside a company

What Is Pangu?
Pangu is Huawei's own family of foundation models, its answer to GPT, Gemini, and Llama.
Rather than one single general-purpose model, Pangu is split into versions built for specific
industries: weather forecasting, drug discovery, finance, and a general-purpose assistant
model, among others. When a Huawei application, or a customer's own custom AI, needs a
foundation model to build on, Pangu is usually the starting point.

3. The 2026 Update: The Agentic Shift
Strategies evolve. At HUAWEI CONNECT 2026, held in Shanghai this very month, Huawei's cloud
story centered on a new phrase: Agentic Hybrid Cloud, Powering a New Era of Intelligence.
Recall yesterday's newest domain, Agentic AI: systems that plan and execute multi-step tasks rather
than answering once and stopping. Huawei's 2026 bet is that enterprises will not run agents on
public cloud alone or on private infrastructure alone, but across a hybrid of both, sensitive data and
low-latency steps kept private, heavy training and bursting handled on public cloud. The broader
ambition behind this is what Huawei calls All Intelligence, extending AI from a handful of tech
companies into every industry and every country, including markets like Pakistan.

Key Insight
Yesterday you learned Agentic AI as one domain among six. Today you see it as a strategy:
Huawei is rebuilding its entire cloud story around it. When a trend moves from one bubble on a
diagram to the theme of a company's flagship conference, that is how you know it is being
taken seriously, not just hyped.

5. ModelArts: Where You'll Actually Build
If EI services are pre-built AI, ModelArts is where you build custom AI. It is Huawei's end-to-end
machine learning platform, comparable to AWS SageMaker, Google Vertex AI, or Azure ML.
● A notebook environment (Jupyter-based) for writing and running code
● Data management and labeling tools
● Training infrastructure you can point at CPU, GPU, or Ascend hardware
● ExeML: AutoML capability for near-codeless model building
● Model versioning and experiment tracking
● One-click deployment to the cloud or to the edge via HiLens

Key Insight
Remember the skills-gap barrier from Day 1? ModelArts is Huawei's direct answer to it. ExeML
lets someone with domain knowledge but limited ML theory produce a working model. Your job
in this course is to understand what is happening under the hood well enough to use the
platform properly and pass the certification, not to become a research scientist in three months.

6. HiLens: AI at the Edge
HiLens is Huawei's platform for running AI directly on a device, a camera, sensor, or piece of
industrial equipment, instead of sending every frame to the cloud for inference.
Why edge matters:
● Latency: real-time decisions cannot wait for a round trip to the cloud
● Bandwidth: streaming video to the cloud around the clock is expensive and often unnecessary
● Privacy: sensitive footage or data may not be allowed to leave the premises
● Reliability: an edge device keeps working even when the internet connection drops

HiLens combines the HiLens Kit, an edge device pairing a camera with an Ascend chip, with a cloud
console (HiLens Studio and Console) used to develop, deploy, and monitor those skills at scale.

Day 1 Connection
Yesterday you learned Edge AI as a trend and Robotics as a domain. HiLens is where those two
intersect. A warehouse robot using HiLens does not send every camera frame to the cloud, it
processes video locally and only reports out when something worth acting on happens.

Key Insight
In short: ModelArts trains the model. HiLens runs it at the edge. You will use both together later
in this course.

Staying Current
This week, at HUAWEI CONNECT 2026, Huawei confirmed a roughly one-generation-per-year
roadmap beyond the 910: Ascend 950 in 2026, Ascend 960 in 2027, and Ascend 970 in 2028,
each roughly doubling compute over the last. This course is built around the established
Ascend and Atlas lineup above, but it is worth knowing the roadmap exists, since the hardware
underneath this entire ecosystem is actively moving.

Remember the pairing: Ascend is the chip, Atlas is the hardware product built around the chip.
When you train a model on ModelArts, you can choose which of this hardware it runs on, and
that choice affects cost, speed, and where the model can ultimately be deployed.

9. Huawei's Global AI Initiatives
Beyond products, Huawei's AI strategy rests on three further pillars.

Talent Development
Huawei partners with governments and training providers worldwide to close the AI skills gap
directly, rather than only hiring already-trained professionals. Its Huawei ICT Academy network alone
partners with over 2,600 universities across more than 110 countries, training over 200,000 students
a year. Local programs delivered through partners such as NAVTTC and Arfa Karim Technology
Incubator, including this course, are part of that same approach: building the skilled workforce a
market needs, in the market itself.

Research Collaboration
Huawei works with universities globally on AI research, particularly around foundation model
development (the Pangu family), Ascend architecture optimization, and industry-specific AI
applications in fields like healthcare, agriculture, and energy.

Open Ecosystem
Despite building its own chips, Huawei keeps the software layer open: ModelArts supports
TensorFlow and PyTorch alongside Huawei's own MindSpore framework, and CANN (the compute
layer that talks to Ascend chips) is community-driven rather than closed.

Why This Matters
On Day 1, you learned the skills gap is one of the four major barriers to AI adoption. Today you
saw how Huawei is actively working to close it, and this program is a direct example. The
companies in your course outline, Huawei, PTA, Jazz, NADRA, SAP, Accenture, and the rest,
are not abstract examples. They are real employers seeking exactly the skills you are building
right now.
